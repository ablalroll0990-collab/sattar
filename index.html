The kernel

    

    
The kernel is a bridge between software and hardware. It receives commands from the software and sends them to the hardware to perform the requested function.

  






  

    
Meet Linus Torvalds

    

      

      

        
In 1991, Linus was a university student with an ordinary computer.

        
He used a system called Minix, the environment he worked in, and started developing his own programs.

        
One of the things he began building was the kernel of Linux, while still a student.

      

    

  






  

    
The GNU project

    

      

      

        
GNU was created by Richard Stallman.

        
He wanted a system just like Unix, but open source.

        
That meant writing everything in the system from scratch. And he did.

        
But one thing was still missing to finish it: the kernel.

      

    

  






  

    
Two projects, one system

    

      

        

        

          
Linus's kernel joined the GNU project, and together they formed the Linux we know.

          
Because it is open source, thousands of developers started improving it.

        

      

      

        

          

          
How did the 30-year-old Linux conquer the world?

        

        
It was only 65 kilobytes and comprised some 10,000 lines of Torvalds' code.

        
The current Linux 5.14 contains over 3.3 million lines of code.

Linux runs the servers

    

      

      

        
Linux is used on a huge number of servers around the world.

        
A surprising paradox: Microsoft uses Linux to run many of its servers instead of its own system, Windows.

        
This shows how efficient Linux is on servers.

      

    

  






  

    
Android is built on Linux

    

      

      

        
Android was built on the Linux kernel.

        
That is why Android is considered partly open source: most of its components are open source.

        
Only some private apps are closed, such as Google Play.

      

    

  






  

    
Linux on Mars

    

      

      

      

        
In 2021, NASA wanted to test whether a helicopter could fly on Mars, where the air is very thin and gravity is lower than on Earth.

        
The shock: it runs on Linux.

      

    

    
Linux is not limited to computers or phones anymore.
